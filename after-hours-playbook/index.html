The After-Hours Playbook

Fix your biggest lead leak tonight · by Marc Pineault, Lightwork · August 2026

This is the whole thing, free. Copy the templates, turn on one setting in the phone system you already pay for, and stop losing the people who call you at 7pm. You don't need to hire anyone and you don't need to buy anything — including from us.

The one-line version: when someone calls and you don't pick up, a text should hit their phone within 60 seconds. That's it. That's the fix. Everything below is how to say it and how to switch it on.

Why after-hours is where the money leaks

Two numbers explain the whole problem. Industry studies consistently find that about 62% of calls to small businesses go unanswered. And 78% of customers buy from whoever responds first.

Put those together. Someone with a burst pipe, a renewal coming up, or a quote to get calls three businesses at 6:40pm. Two of them ring out. One texts back in a minute. That one gets the job — not because they're better, but because they were there.

You already paid for that call. You paid for the ad, the truck wrap, the referral you earned five years ago. Letting it ring out is the most expensive thing in your business, and it's also the cheapest thing to fix.

The missed-call text-back

Three templates. Use them exactly, or swap the words for how you actually talk. Replace anything in [square brackets].

Rules that make them work: keep them short, sign the business name so they know who's texting, ask one question, and never send between 9pm and 8am local time. A text at 11:15pm doesn't read as helpful — it reads as desperate, and in some places it's a compliance problem. Hold the late ones until 8am.

1. Missed a call during business hours

Send within 60 seconds of the missed call
Hi, it's [BUSINESS NAME] — sorry we missed you just now.
We're on a job and can't pick up. What do you need help
with? Reply here and we'll sort it out.
Why it works: it explains the silence, so they don't assume you're closed or don't care. And "reply here" turns a dead call into a conversation you can answer at a red light.

2. Missed a call after hours

Send when the call comes in outside your hours (but not 9pm–8am)
Hi, it's [BUSINESS NAME]. You called after hours so we
missed you. Send us a quick note on what you need and
we'll call you back [FIRST THING TOMORROW / MONDAY MORNING].
If it's urgent tonight, text URGENT and we'll get to you.
Why it works: it sets a real callback time so they stop shopping, and the URGENT word gives true emergencies a path without putting your cell number on the internet. If you don't do emergency work, delete that last line — don't promise it.

3. The quote that went quiet

Send 3–5 days after a quote with no answer
Hi [FIRST NAME], [YOUR NAME] at [BUSINESS NAME]. Checking
in on the quote we sent for [THE JOB] — still something
you're looking at, or has it moved to the back burner?
Either answer is fine, just don't want to chase you.
Why it works: "either answer is fine" removes the pressure that makes people ghost you. You'll get a yes, a not-yet, or a no — and all three beat wondering. Send this to people who asked you for a quote. It's a follow-up to a conversation they started, which is exactly what these templates are for.
One compliance note, quickly: everything here is for replying to people who contacted you — they called, they messaged, they asked for a quote. That's solicited, and it's fine in Canada and the US. Texting a list of people who never contacted you is a different thing with different rules, and it's not in this playbook. Also: put your business name in the first text so nobody has to guess who's messaging them.

How to turn it on

You almost certainly already have the tools. Pick the ones that match how people reach you and set them up in one sitting.

Your phone provider

If you're on a VoIP or business phone service, look in the admin settings for "missed call text-back," "auto-reply to missed calls," "SMS auto-response," or a webhook on the missed-call event. Most business phone services built this in over the last few years and most owners never switched it on. Search your provider's help docs for "missed call auto text" — it's usually a toggle plus a message box you paste template 1 into.

If your provider offers separate schedules for business hours and after hours, use both messages: template 1 during the day, template 2 at night. If it only allows one message, use a blended version that doesn't promise a same-day callback.

If your provider can't do it

Two workarounds that don't require changing your number:

  • Conditional call forwarding — your carrier can forward only the calls you don't answer (busy or no-answer) to a second number that can text back. Your main line stays exactly as it is; you're just catching the overflow.
  • A cheap second line that handles the texting, forwarded from the calls you miss. Costs a few dollars a month.

Either way, test it: call your own number from a different phone, let it ring out, and time how long the text takes. If it doesn't arrive in a minute, it isn't working.

Instagram and Facebook DMs

In the Instagram app, open your professional account settings and find Saved Replies (also called Quick Replies). Save template 2 with a shortcut like ah so you can fire it with two taps from bed. Instagram also has an away message for business accounts that sends automatically outside your set hours — turn that on with template 2 and you're covered without touching your phone.

One warning worth knowing: social platforms generally only let you reply inside a 24-hour window after someone messages you. A DM that sits all weekend can become unanswerable. If your leads come through DMs, that away message isn't optional.

Google Business Profile

If messaging is turned on for your Google listing, set a welcome message in the messaging settings — that's the first thing someone sees when they tap "Message" from search or Maps. Use a short version of template 2. And check the messages tab actually notifies you; a listing that shows "typically replies in a day" costs you the exact customers this playbook is about.

If you don't want to monitor it, turn Google messaging off rather than leaving it unanswered. An unanswered channel is worse than a closed one.

The after-hours voicemail script

Most voicemail greetings are a dead end: "leave a message and we'll get back to you." Nobody believes it and half of callers hang up. Rewrite yours to point at text, word for word:

Record this — after-hours greeting
You've reached [BUSINESS NAME]. We're closed right now,
so the fastest way to reach us is to text this same
number — we read texts first thing in the morning and
we'll get back to you [BY 9AM / SAME DAY].

If you'd rather leave a message, go ahead after the tone
and include your name, your number, and the address or
job you're calling about. Thanks for calling.
Why it works: it gives a faster option than waiting, and asking for name, number and job up front means the voicemails you do get are actually workable instead of "hey, call me back."

Record it in your own voice, not a robot one. Say the callback time out loud and then actually hit it — a promise you keep on Monday morning is worth more than any script on this page.

Response-time rules of thumb

Time to first responseWhat it does
Under 5 minutesThe target. You're almost always the first responder, and the conversation is still live in their head.
5 to 60 minutesStill fine — if an auto-text already landed. The text holds your place in line while you finish the job you're on.
Same dayWorkable for quotes and non-urgent work. Not workable for anything with water, heat, or a deadline in it.
Next day or laterAssume they've already called someone else. Call anyway, but don't count it.

The point of the text-back is not to answer the question. It's to buy you time. Five seconds of automation converts "they didn't pick up" into "they're on it" — and that's the entire gap between you and the competitor who answered.

Do this tonight

  1. Call your own business number from another phone and let it ring out. Notice what happens: probably nothing.
  2. Turn on missed-call auto-text in your phone provider, or set up the forwarding workaround.
  3. Paste in template 1 for hours, template 2 for after hours. Put your business name in both.
  4. Set the quiet window so nothing sends 9pm–8am.
  5. Re-record your voicemail with the script above.
  6. Save template 2 as an Instagram saved reply and turn the away message on.
  7. Test it again. Time the text. If it lands in under a minute, you're done.

Total cost: about 40 minutes and whatever you already pay for your phone.
