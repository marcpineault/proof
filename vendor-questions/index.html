The Vendor BS Detector

17 questions · by Marc Pineault, Lightwork · London, Ontario

We sell AI systems. So read this with the appropriate suspicion — and then use it on us.

These are the questions we would want a business owner to ask us before signing anything, because they're the ones that separate a system built into your business from a template someone touched once. Each one comes with the answer that should end the conversation.

How to use it: ask all 17 on the call, out loud, and write down the answers. You are not looking for perfect responses — you're looking for a vendor who has clearly thought about the question before. Hesitation on ownership, compliance, or what happens when it breaks is the whole signal.
Section 1 of 5

Proof

Anyone can demo software. Very few run it on themselves.

01

Show me it running on your own business. Right now.

If the system is good enough to sell you, it's good enough for their own phone and inbox. A vendor who won't eat their own cooking is reselling someone else's software.

Walk away if"That's not really applicable to what we do." Translation: they've never lived with the thing they're about to install in your business.
02

Whose numbers are on your website?

Most agency case studies are borrowed — from the software platform they resell, from a white-label partner, or from a client of a client.

Walk away ifThey can't tell you which business produced a result, or the "results" are screenshots they can't reproduce live in the account they came from.
03

Can I hear a real conversation it handled — not a demo script?

Demos are rehearsed on the happy path. Real transcripts show you how it behaves when a caller is angry, confused, or asking something nobody anticipated.

Walk away ifThe only thing they can show you is a polished demo reel, or a sandbox number that only answers the two questions they scripted.
04

Who actually builds mine, and will that person still be around in month three?

Plenty of shops sell with a closer and deliver with a template. You want to know the name of the person configuring your system and who answers when it misbehaves.

Walk away ifThe salesperson can't name the builder, or "onboarding" means you get a login and a help-doc link.
Section 2 of 5

Ownership

Assume the relationship ends badly. What do you walk out with?

05

Who owns the phone number — and can I port it out?

Your number is on your trucks, your invoices, and every review site. If the vendor holds it, they hold your business.

Walk away if"We provision it on our account." That's a hostage, not a feature. Porting should be a yes with no conditions attached.
06

Who owns the conversation data and the lead records?

Every call, text, and DM the system handles is a record of who wanted to buy from you and why. That is your asset, not theirs.

Walk away ifExport is "available on request," limited to a summary report, or only offered while your account is in good standing.
07

Do I get the prompts, scripts, and automation logic?

The instructions that tell the AI how to speak for your business are your voice, written down. You should be able to read them, edit them, and take them with you.

Walk away if"That's proprietary." Sometimes there's real IP in the platform — but the words your customers hear should never be a black box you're not allowed to open.
08

Whose name is on the accounts — and whose credit card?

Calendars, CRM, texting, email sending. If those live under the vendor's master account, you don't have a system, you have a subscription to their setup.

Walk away ifEverything sits inside their sub-account and they can't hand you admin. Ask directly: "if we part ways Friday, what still works Monday?"
Section 3 of 5

Compliance

If it texts your customers, someone is carrying legal risk. Find out who.

09

Who handles the business texting registration — and is it under my name?

Carriers require businesses to register before sending application-to-person texts. Unregistered traffic gets filtered or blocked, which means your follow-ups silently stop arriving.

Walk away if"Don't worry about it," or "we just use a regular cell number." Both are ways of saying your messages will start disappearing and nobody will notice.
10

How do you handle consent and opt-outs — CASL in Canada, TCPA in the US?

Automated outreach makes it very easy to message people you never had permission to message, at scale, with a timestamped record of doing it.

Walk away ifThey don't recognise the acronyms, or the answer is "everyone does this." A serious vendor can tell you where consent is captured, where it's stored, and how a STOP is honoured everywhere at once.
11

What are the quiet hours, whose time zone, and who can change them?

Automations fire on schedules. Without an explicit quiet-hours rule, a follow-up sequence will eventually text a customer at 3am — and that customer will remember it.

Walk away ifThere are no quiet hours, or the schedule runs in the vendor's time zone instead of your customers'.
Section 4 of 5

Uptime & safety

Everything breaks. The only question is whether anyone finds out before your customers do.

12

It's Saturday at 7pm and it stops answering. Who finds out, and how?

A silent failure is worse than no system at all — you've stopped answering the phone and you don't know it. Weekends and holidays are exactly when it matters.

Walk away if"It never goes down." Everything goes down. The real answer names a monitor, a threshold, and a human who gets paged. If the answer is "you'd let us know Monday," you are the monitoring.
13

What can the AI do without a human approving it?

There's a real difference between a system that books an appointment and one that can quote a price, promise a timeline, or commit you to work you can't do.

Walk away ifThere's no list. A good vendor can tell you exactly which actions are automatic, which are gated behind your approval, and how to move an item from one bucket to the other.
14

What's the escalation path to a human — and how fast does it trigger?

Some conversations should never be handled by software: complaints, emergencies, a big client with an odd request. The system needs to know when to tap out.

Walk away if"The AI handles 100% of conversations." That's not a capability claim, it's a description of a system that will annoy the customers you least want annoyed.
Section 5 of 5

Money & exit

A vendor who won't be measured is a vendor who plans not to be.

15

What single number do you report to me every month?

Activity is easy to fake and easy to inflate. You want one outcome number tied to money — booked jobs, qualified conversations, recovered enquiries — reported the same way every month.

Walk away ifThe answer is a dashboard tour. Messages sent, minutes handled, and "engagement" are not results. If nobody names a number, nobody is accountable to one.
16

And what happens if that number drops?

This is the question that reveals whether you're buying a service or renting software. Something specific has to happen when the system underperforms.

Walk away if"Give it more time." A real answer sounds like: we look at it, we tell you what we changed, and here's what we do if it stays down.
17

Walk me through leaving. What's the notice, and what breaks?

Ask this on the first call, cheerfully, before any money moves. How a vendor describes the exit tells you exactly how confident they are that you'll want to stay.

Walk away ifA twelve-month lock-in on an unproven system, an exit fee, or the honest-but-fatal admission that if you leave, your phone number, your booking flow, and your customer history all go with them.
