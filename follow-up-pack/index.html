The Follow-Up Pack

7 touches, written out · by Marc Pineault, Lightwork · August 2026

Most sales don't die from rejection. They die from silence. Someone calls, fills in a form, or sends a DM — you reply once, they get busy, and nobody says anything again. The job goes to whoever kept talking.

This is the exact 7-touch sequence our own machine runs on every new enquiry. Every message is written out below. Copy them into your phone, your email, or your CRM and run it by hand. It works either way — the sequence is the asset, not the software.

How to use this: the moment a new enquiry comes in, start the clock at Day 0. Send each touch on its day. The second they reply, stop the sequence and have a real conversation. That's the whole system.

Seven touches over three weeks. Total hands-on time if you run it manually: about 12 minutes per enquiry.

The messages

Swap anything in [square brackets] for your own details. Keep them short. Short reads as human; long reads as a campaign.

T1 — Day 0, instant text

Job: prove a human is on it

Text · send within 5 minutesHi [First name] — [Your name] here from [Business name]. Got your [call / message / enquiry] about [what they asked about]. I'm on it. Quick one so I can help properly: [one specific question — e.g. what's the address? / when were you hoping to start?] You can reply right here.

Why it works: it names you, names the business, repeats what they asked for so they know you actually read it, and asks exactly one question. One question gets answered. Three get ignored.

T2 — Day 0, email

Job: deliver the useful thing

Email · subject line[What they asked about] — here's what you asked for
Email · bodyHi [First name], Good to hear from you today. Here's the [quote / info / availability / pricing] I mentioned: [The actual useful thing. A number, a date, a straight answer. Not "let's book a call to discuss."] Two things worth knowing before you decide: 1. [Something genuinely helpful, even if it doesn't sell for you — e.g. "if you can wait until spring, the price drops about 15%."] 2. [The next practical step and how long it takes.] If it's helpful, the easiest next step is [book a time here / reply with a day that works]. If the timing's off, just say so and I'll get out of your inbox. [Your name] [Business name] · [phone] · [website]

Why it works: it hands over real information instead of holding it hostage behind a call. The point that doesn't sell for you is the point they remember.

T3 — Day 2, text

Job: one light nudge

TextHi [First name], [Your name] from [Business name] — did that [quote / info] land okay? No rush either way. Just tell me yes, not yet, or no and I'll take it from there.

Why it works: "yes, not yet, or no" gives permission to say no, which is why people answer. A no on Day 2 is worth more than silence for a month.

T4 — Day 5, email

Job: answer the #1 objection

Email · subject lineThe part people usually ask me about
Email · bodyHi [First name], Not chasing — just answering the question most people have at this stage and don't always want to ask. "[The real objection. Usually: is it worth the money? how long will it take? what if it goes wrong?]" Straight answer: [Answer it honestly, including the part that isn't flattering. E.g. "it's not the cheapest option in town. Here's what the extra buys you, and here's when the cheaper option is genuinely the right call."] [One short, real example: a customer, what they were worried about, what actually happened. Real or leave it out.] If that changes things, reply and we'll sort out timing. If not, no hard feelings. [Your name] [Business name] · [phone]

Why it works: naming the objection out loud takes the awkwardness out of it. Admitting a weakness makes everything else you said more believable.

T5 — Day 9, phone call

Job: be a voice, not a notification

Call once. If they pick up, forget the script — ask how the [project / decision] is going and listen. If it goes to voicemail, leave this. Keep it under 20 seconds.

Voicemail scriptHi [First name], it's [Your name] from [Business name] — you got in touch about [what they asked about]. No pressure at all. I'm just closing off my list for the month and wanted to check whether you still want a hand with it, or whether it's sorted. Either way is fine. Give me a shout at [phone number], or reply to my text — that's easier. Thanks [First name].
Text · send right after the voicemailJust left you a voicemail — [Your name] at [Business name]. Replying here is easier if you'd rather.

Why it works: hardly anyone calls anymore, which is exactly why it lands. The follow-up text stops them wondering who the missed number was.

T6 — Day 14, email

Job: the honest case for acting now

Email · subject lineWorth knowing if you're still deciding
Email · bodyHi [First name], Two weeks since you got in touch, so I'll be straight with you. Waiting on [the thing they enquired about] usually costs something. In your case it's probably [the real, specific cost of waiting — e.g. "another season of the same problem," "prices go up in October," "the fix gets bigger the longer it sits."] And sometimes waiting is the right call. If [the honest condition — e.g. "you're moving next year," or "cash is tight right now"], then wait. I'd rather you did the right thing than the fast thing. I've got [real availability — e.g. "two slots left in September"]. If you want one, reply and it's yours. If not, this is my second-to-last email and I'll leave you be. [Your name] [Business name] · [phone]

Why it works: it gives a real reason to move and a real reason not to. Only claim scarcity you actually have — a fake deadline is the fastest way to lose someone who was going to buy in March.

T7 — Day 21, text

Job: leave the door open

Text · the open loopHi [First name] — [Your name] at [Business name]. Last one from me, promise. I'll assume [the thing] is handled or parked for now. Either is completely fine. If it comes back around — this winter, next year, whenever — text this number and I'll pick it straight back up. Nothing to re-explain. All the best.

Why it works: it ends the chase without ending the relationship. This message gets more replies than the six before it, because it's the only one that asks for nothing.

The rules

The sequence is the easy part. These rules are what keep it from turning into spam — and keep you on the right side of the people you're trying to win.

Non-negotiables

  1. Stop the moment they reply. Any reply, any channel — the sequence is over. Nothing kills goodwill faster than an automated Day 5 email landing after a real conversation on Day 4.
  2. Never text before 8am or after 9pm in their local time. Not their timezone — theirs. A 7:40am text reads as desperate; a 10pm text reads as rude.
  3. Every message identifies your business. Name and business in every text and every email, every time. If they have to ask "who is this?", you've already lost the thread.
  4. If they say stop, stop everywhere, forever. "Stop," "no thanks," "not interested," "remove me" — all the same thing. Take them off texts and email and the call list, immediately and permanently. One list, not three.
  5. One sender, one thread. Same phone number, same email address, start to finish. Rotating numbers is what spammers do and customers can tell.
  6. Seven and done. When the sequence ends, it ends. Moving someone to a monthly newsletter they never asked for is how you get marked as spam.
Consent, in one line: these templates are written for people who contacted you first — an enquiry, a form, a call, a DM. That's the whole scope of this pack. In Canada, CASL governs commercial electronic messages including texts and DMs, and an enquiry generally gives you a limited window of implied consent. In the US, the TCPA governs marketing texts and calls and generally expects prior express written consent for marketing messages, plus honouring do-not-call and opt-out requests. This is a plain-English pointer, not legal advice — if you're running this at volume or reaching people who never contacted you, talk to a lawyer who does this for a living.

The honest bit

Running this by hand takes discipline. Not skill — discipline. Touch 1 is easy. Touch 5 on a Thursday when you're on a roof, three jobs behind, and the enquiry is nine days cold is where almost everyone quits. That's not a character flaw, it's just how busy weeks work. Our machine has sent 7,792 of these follow-ups without missing one — that's the only number on this page, and it's ours, pulled from our production database. We're not going to quote you an industry statistic to make the case. Run the sequence by hand for a month and you'll see your own numbers, which are the ones that matter.
